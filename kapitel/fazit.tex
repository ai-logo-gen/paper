\section{Conclusion and Outlook}\label{sec:fazit}

This work set out to answer whether foundation models can effectively master simplicity through resource-efficient specialization. The investigation into multimodal logo generation has demonstrated that by combining LoRA-based fine-tuning with ControlNet guidance, a general-purpose model can be successfully adapted to the highly specific and constrained domain of minimalist design using consumer-grade hardware.

The experimental evaluation confirmed that while foundation models provide a powerful base, specialized "mastery" is achieved through multimodal conditioning. Structural guidance proved indispensable for geometric precision (SSIM +31.9\%), while targeted fine-tuning significantly enhanced stylistic coherence (FID -31.0\%). This confirms that complexity can be harnessed for simplicity without requiring industrial-scale compute resources.

\subsection{Scientific Contributions and Implications}
A key contribution of this work is the validation of parameter-efficient training strategies for the specific domain of logo design.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Hyperparameter Dynamics:} The findings confirm literature recommendations for high learning rates ($1e-4$) in PEFT methods \cite{HU2021}. However, contrary to the suggestion that very low ranks (4 or 8) constitute a "sweet spot" \cite{HU2021}, the experiments demonstrated that a higher rank of 32 provided the necessary capacity to capture the stylistic nuances of minimalist design without overfitting.
    \item \textbf{Efficiency Verification:} It was demonstrated that a compact dataset of 1,500 images and approximately 2,500 training steps are sufficient to produce highly compelling results in the context of minimalist logo generation with sketch and text input. These results are viable for commercial applications, particularly in early-stage ideation, and challenge the general assumption that massive datasets are a mandatory prerequisite for effective domain adaptation.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Future Directions}
Future optimization should prioritize data quality over quantity. The integration of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) offers a promising avenue to replace simplistic tag-based captions with dense, descriptive natural language, significantly improving semantic alignment. Furthermore, advances in synthetic data generation and evolving model architectures suggest that the barrier to entry for training specialized, high-quality generative models will continue to decrease.
Generative AI establishes itself not as a replacement for human expertise, but as a powerful instrument for exploration and iteration, democratizing access to professional design tools.
