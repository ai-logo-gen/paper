\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
Minimalist logo design relies on reduction, clarity, and structural precision - qualities that are often challenging for general purpose generative models to achieve consistently. While large-scale text-to-image models excel at artistic composition, they frequently struggle to produce the clean, vector-like aesthetics required for professional branding or to strictly adhere to a user's layout constraints \cite[1]{bertao2023blind}.

This work addresses a fundamental question in the era of large-scale generative models: \textit{Can foundation models master simplicity?} While industrial trends favor increasing model complexity and parameters, this research investigates the effectiveness of resource-efficient specialization for the task of minimalist logo design. By fine-tuning Stable Diffusion v1.5 \cite{HuggingFace_StableDiffusionv15_ModelCard}, a pipeline is presented that balances semantic relevance with strict structural control.

A hybrid multimodal conditioning strategy is employed: text prompts define the stylistic intent, while ControlNet \cite{ZHANG2023} enforces geometric structure based on rough sketches. Unlike approaches requiring massive datasets, this focus is on whether a general-purpose model can be adapted to a "simpler" but highly constrained domain using limited computational resources (NVIDIA RTX 5080) and a compact, high-quality dataset of 1,500 examples.

The research focuses on the optimization of this fine-tuning process. A rigorous analysis of Hyperparameters within the Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) technique is conducted, identifying the optimal configurations for achieving stylistic coherence and structural fidelity on consumer-grade hardware.

The evaluation benchmarks the model's performance through Fréchet Inception Distance (FID), CLIP score, and Structural Similarity Index (SSIM). These metrics, alongside qualitative case studies, investigate whether efficiency and specialization can indeed overcome the limitations of foundation models in mastering minimalist design.

\subsection{Hypotheses}
Based on the defined objectives, the following hypotheses are postulated:
\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Hypothesis 1 (H1):} Additional conditioning of the model via a sketch leads to a significantly higher structural correspondence with the design intent compared to purely text-conditioned results, without loss of image quality.
	\item \textbf{Hypothesis 2 (H2):} Optimizing an image generator through fine-tuning on a specialized logo dataset significantly improves the ability to generate stylistically coherent logos, as reflected in established image quality metrics.
	\item \textbf{Hypothesis 3 (H3):} Specific hyperparameter configurations have a direct and measurable influence on result quality, with an optimal configuration leading to better visual quality, structural correspondence, and text-image coherence.
	\item \textbf{Hypothesis 4 (H4):} A resource-efficiently optimized prototype generates results on consumer-grade hardware that exhibit higher quality in realizing design intent and greater commercial relevance in a qualitative evaluation compared to the unspecialized base model.
\end{itemize}
